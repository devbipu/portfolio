problems:
  heading: Why Most WordPress Sites End Up Slow and Fragile
  intro: >-
    Almost every WordPress rescue job I see has the same handful of causes. The
    platform is rarely the problem.
  items:
    - A page builder adding seconds to every page load
    - Thirty plugins installed, six of them doing the same job
    - A premium theme whose updates overwrite your changes
    - Editing a simple paragraph requires a developer
    - Images uploaded straight off a phone at full resolution
    - No backups, or backups nobody has ever tested restoring
    - A plugin left unpatched for two years on a public site
    - Contact form submissions going to an inbox nobody opens
    - Rankings that slid after a redesign nobody checked redirects for
  note: >-
    These are all preventable. Most of them come from decisions made to save a
    week at the start of the project.

faq:
  heading: Questions
  questions:
    - title: Will I be able to edit the site myself?
      answer: >-
        Yes - that is usually the main reason to choose WordPress. Pages are
        built from blocks and fields so you can change copy, images and
        sections, and add new pages, without being able to break the layout.
    - title: Can you redesign my site without losing search rankings?
      answer: >-
        That is what the redirect map is for. Existing URLs are inventoried
        before launch and mapped to their new equivalents, so the pages
        currently earning traffic keep their value instead of returning 404s.
    - title: Can you rescue a hacked or broken site?
      answer: >-
        Often, yes. That starts with getting it to a safe state and assessing
        the damage. Sometimes a clean rebuild on the existing content is faster
        and safer than cleaning an infected install, and I will tell you which.
    - title: Can you remove Elementor, Divi or WPBakery?
      answer: >-
        Yes, by rebuilding the templates as a proper theme and converting the
        content. It is the single biggest page-speed win on most sites, but it
        is real work - the effort depends on how many unique layouts exist.
    - title: Do you set up WooCommerce?
      answer: >-
        Yes - products, variations, shipping, tax, checkout and the payment
        gateway you already use, styled to match the site. Existing products
        can usually be migrated from a spreadsheet or another platform.
    - title: Which plugins will I be paying for?
      answer: >-
        As few as possible, and you will know the list before launch. A couple
        of commercial plugins are genuinely worth their renewal; most of what
        gets installed on a typical site is not.
    - title: Do you handle hosting?
      answer: >-
        I will recommend hosting that suits the site and help you set it up,
        but the account stays in your name. You should never be in a position
        where your developer controls access to your own website.
    - title: Can you keep maintaining it after launch?
      answer: >-
        Yes - updates, backups, monitoring and small content or feature changes
        can be arranged on an ongoing basis. If you would rather handle it
        in-house, the routine is documented so your team can.
